# Session 1 — Why Coding

- **Program:** Chiang Mai Mission School AI & Coding Program (School Track, English-language)
- **Session:** 1 of 4 — Week 1: Why Coding
- **Duration:** 60 minutes
- **Format:** Unplugged (no computers/devices)
- **Students:** 9 total — Nant Team (5) and Immanuel Team (4), fixed teams
- **Age range:** G3–G8 (int'l) / 중2–중3 (KR) — design for youngest, keep engaging for oldest

## Learning Goal

- Computers follow instructions.
- Understand what an algorithm is.
- Learn that clear instructions are important.

## Vocabulary

| Word | Simple meaning for class |
|---|---|
| Algorithm | A set of steps to solve a problem or finish a task |
| Instruction | One thing you tell someone (or something) to do |
| Step | One part of a bigger set of instructions |
| Order | The sequence steps happen in — order matters! |
| Computer | A machine that follows instructions exactly, without guessing |

## Materials

- [ ] Jam, bread, butter knife (or plastic knife), plate — for the teacher's "robot" demo
- [ ] Whiteboard / large paper to record each team's instruction list
- [ ] Markers, one set per team
- [ ] Name tags or team badges (Nant Team / Immanuel Team)
- [ ] Timer (visible to students)

## Activity Details

### 1–2. Ice Breaking & Robot Naming (Individual, 10 min)

**Goal:** Warm up the room, get every student to speak at least once, and set up the teacher as the "robot" character used later in the Jam Sandwich activity.

**How to run it:**
- Teacher introduces self.
- Quick round: each student says their name and one favorite thing.
- Teacher announces: "Today I'm going to be a robot for one of our activities. Help me pick my robot name!" Each student calls out one idea individually; class picks one by quick show of hands.

**Note:** Keep this fast and light — it's a warm-up, not the main lesson. The robot name should stay fun/silly to keep the youngest students engaged.

### 3. Why Coding? Q&A (Individual, 8 min)

**Goal:** Build the idea that coding/algorithms are everywhere, before introducing the word "algorithm" formally.

**How to run it:** Ask the whole group, taking individual answers:
- "Where do you think coding is used?"
- Guide toward: **Robots**, **Games**, **AI**, **Everyday life**
- Introduce the words **Algorithm**, **Instruction**, **Step**, **Order**, **Computer** briefly — write them on the board, no need to explain deeply yet, they'll be used hands-on next.

### 4–10. Jam Sandwich Algorithm (Team-based)

**Goal:** Let each team discover — by watching their own instructions fail and then succeed — that computers (and the "robot" teacher) never guess, and that clear, ordered instructions are what make an algorithm work.

**Framing note:** This is collaborative and comparative between the two teams, **not a competition**. Do not score or rank the teams — both teams are expected to fail on Attempt 1 and succeed on the Retry. Frame it as "two teams learning the same lesson together," not "who wins."

**Step 4 — Team Huddle (5 min, both teams work at the same time):**
- Each team gathers separately (teacher circulates between both).
- Every team member must contribute at least one step for making a jam sandwich.
- The team negotiates and combines individual steps into a single, agreed instruction list, written down by one team member.

**Step 5 — Attempt 1: Nant Team directs the Robot (5 min):**
- Nant Team reads their instructions aloud, one step at a time.
- Teacher-as-robot follows the instructions **literally**, deliberately failing at any vague or ambiguous step (e.g. "put the jam on the bread" → robot dumps the whole jar on top).
- Let the "failure" be visible and a little funny — this is the point, not a mistake to feel bad about.

**Step 6 — Attempt 1: Immanuel Team directs the Robot (5 min):**
- Same process as Step 5, with Immanuel Team's instructions.

**Step 7 — "Why Did It Fail?" Quick Discussion (3 min, whole group):**
- Ask both teams together: "What happened? Why did the robot do that?"
- Draw out: the robot didn't guess what you meant — it only did exactly what you said.

**Step 8 — Revise Instructions (3 min, both teams work at the same time):**
- Each team goes back to their instruction list and fixes the vague steps, based on what they just saw fail.

**Step 9 — Retry: Nant Team (3 min):**
- Nant Team gives their revised instructions; teacher-as-robot follows them and (this time) succeeds.

**Step 10 — Retry: Immanuel Team (3 min):**
- Same for Immanuel Team.

### 11. Whole-Group Debrief (5 min)

**Goal:** Connect the hands-on experience explicitly to the lesson's core idea.

**Discussion prompts:**
- "What changed between your first try and your second try?"
- "What made the second set of instructions work?"
- Land on: clear, ordered, specific instructions = an algorithm. Vague instructions fail because the computer (or robot) never guesses.

### 12. Reflection (Individual, 5 min)

Ask students to answer briefly (verbally or on paper):
- What was difficult about giving instructions?
- Why did the robot/computer fail the first time?

### 13. Closing & Takeaway (5 min)

State and reinforce the takeaway together:

> **Computers never guess. Good instructions make good results.**

Preview: "Next session, we'll learn how computers make choices and repeat steps."

## Reference / Risks

- Widen or narrow vocabulary explanation depth live depending on how the group responds — G3 students may need the words repeated more; older students (G7/G8, 중2/중3) may get restless if the vocabulary section runs long, so keep it to definitions + one example each.
- If a team's Attempt 1 accidentally succeeds (instructions happen to be clear enough), don't force a "fail" — instead ask the team what made it work, and use that as the teaching moment instead of the Retry round.
- Keep the "robot" failures visibly playful, not embarrassing — this matters especially for the youngest students in the room.
- If Team Huddle runs long because ideas conflict, the teacher should step in at the 5-minute mark and ask the team to vote on the remaining disagreement rather than letting it run over.

---

**Time check:** Segments 1–13 sum to 5+5+8+5+5+5+3+3+3+3+5+5+5 = **60 min ✅**

**Trims made to fit the hour:** The full team-based Jam Sandwich sequence (huddle → attempt 1 ×2 teams → discussion → revise → retry ×2 teams → debrief) totals 32 minutes on its own — too long to run sequentially alongside Ice Breaking/Robot Naming/Q&A (18 min) and Reflection/Closing (10 min) within 60 minutes. To make it fit without cutting content: (1) **Team Huddle and Revise Instructions run in parallel** for both teams simultaneously (teacher circulates) instead of one team at a time, and (2) the **"why did it fail?" discussion is held once, together, after both teams' Attempt 1**, instead of repeating it separately per team. No individual activity (Ice Breaking, Robot Naming, Q&A) was shortened.
