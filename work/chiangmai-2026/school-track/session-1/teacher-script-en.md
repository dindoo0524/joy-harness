# Session 1 — Why Coding: Teacher Script (Spoken)

- **Program:** Chiang Mai Mission School AI & Coding Program (School Track, English-language)
- **Session:** 1 of 4 — Week 1: Why Coding
- **Duration:** 60 minutes
- **Teams:** Nant Team (5) and Immanuel Team (4) — fixed teams
- **How to use this script:** This is a spoken-word script, not a summary. Read it out loud during rehearsal. Lines in *italics inside brackets* are stage directions, not spoken lines. `[Robot Name]` is a placeholder — the real name is chosen live by students in Segment 2, so swap it in from that point on.

---

## 1. Opening & Ice Breaking (5 min)

**TEACHER:**
Hello, everyone! My name is Joy. I'm so happy to meet you today.

I'm from Korea. To come here and meet you, I took an airplane and traveled a long way to reach you.

Thank you so much for having me here for these four days of AI and coding class. I really hope this class makes you think, makes you smile, and gives you a great feeling of "I did it!" by the end.

*[Smile, look around the room, make eye contact with a few students.]*

Now, let's get to know each other a little bit.

*(Quick round: each student says their name and one favorite thing.)*

**TEACHER:** Great! Now I know a little bit about everyone. Let's start our adventure together!

---

## 2. Robot Naming (5 min)

**TEACHER:**
Today, I'm going to be a robot for one of our activities!

*[Act like a robot for a second — stiff arms, flat voice — to get a laugh.]*

But a robot needs a name, right? Let's give this robot a name together!

*(Each student calls out one idea. The class picks one by a quick show of hands.)*

**TEACHER:** Okay! From now on, this robot's name is **[Robot Name]**. Let's give [Robot Name] a big round of applause!

*[Clap together, keep the energy up.]*

We're going to use [Robot Name] later today for a special mission — so keep that name in your head!

---

## 3. Why Coding? Q&A (8 min)

**TEACHER:** Now, let me ask you a question. Do you know what "coding" is?

*(Wait — let a few students answer first.)*

**TEACHER:** Good guesses! Yes — coding makes a robot move, calculate, and do things for us. Coding is how we tell a computer, or a robot, exactly what to do.

Why do we need coding? We want to use the robot's power, and let it do some work for us instead of doing it ourselves.

Now — where do we use coding in real life? Where do you think coding is used?

*(Take a few answers — guide toward: elevators, phones, games, computers, AI.)*

**TEACHER:** Yes! Things like elevators and computers — they are all programmed by coding. Coding is everywhere around us, and we need the robot's power more and more every day. That's why it's so useful to learn how robots work, and how to use their power.

*[Write on the board: Algorithm, Instruction, Step, Order, Computer.]*

**TEACHER:** Let's remember a few new words today:

- **Algorithm** — a set of steps to solve a problem or finish a task.
- **Instruction** — one thing you tell someone, or something, to do.
- **Step** — one part of a bigger set of instructions.
- **Order** — the sequence steps happen in. Order matters!
- **Computer** — a machine that follows instructions exactly, without guessing.

We're going to use these words a lot today. Now, let's put them into action!

Today, we're going to use [Robot Name] to do a job for us — making a strawberry jam sandwich! But there's one important rule: [Robot Name] can only do exactly what you tell it. Nothing more, nothing less.

You're going to work in two teams: **Nant Team** and **Immanuel Team**. Each team is going to give [Robot Name] instructions, one step at a time — and I will be your robot, [Robot Name].

Are you ready? Let's start!

---

## 4. Jam Sandwich — Team Huddle (5 min, both teams work at the same time)

**TEACHER:** First, each team needs a huddle. Get together with your team.

Every single person on the team must give at least one step for making a jam sandwich. Then, put all of your ideas together and agree on one instruction list. One person writes it down for the team.

*[Teacher circulates between both teams, listens in, gives light encouragement — but does not correct or fix the instructions yet.]*

**TEACHER (circulating):** You're doing great. Remember — [Robot Name] can't guess. Try to think about every small detail.

*(At the 5-minute mark, call time. If a team is stuck arguing, step in and ask them to vote on the last disagreement.)*

**TEACHER:** Time's up! Let's see who goes first.

---

## 5. Jam Sandwich — Attempt 1: Nant Team Directs the Robot (5 min)

**TEACHER:** Nant Team, you're up first! Please read your instructions to [Robot Name], one step at a time. I will follow exactly what you say — nothing more, nothing less.

*[Teacher becomes the robot: stiff movements, flat "robot voice," follows every instruction completely literally. Deliberately fails at a vague step.]*

**NANT TEAM:** Step one — pick up the bread.

*[Teacher picks up the bread.]*

**TEACHER (as robot):** Beep. Bread — picked up.

**NANT TEAM:** Step two — put jam on the bread.

*[Teacher takes the whole jam jar and sets it directly on top of the closed bread bag — technically "jam on the bread," but clearly not what anyone meant.]*

**TEACHER (as robot):** Beep. Jam is on the bread. Task complete!

*[Hold up the result — clearly wrong, a little funny. Let students react.]*

**TEACHER (breaking character):** Uh-oh! Is this what you wanted?

*(Students react — laugh, say no, point out the problem.)*

**TEACHER:** Okay, Nant Team, thank you! Let's remember this moment. Now, let's give Immanuel Team a turn.

---

## 6. Jam Sandwich — Attempt 1: Immanuel Team Directs the Robot (5 min)

**TEACHER:** Immanuel Team, it's your turn now! Same rule — I will follow only what you say, exactly as you say it.

**IMMANUEL TEAM:** Step one — take a slice of bread.

*[Teacher picks up one slice of bread.]*

**TEACHER (as robot):** Beep. Bread — ready.

**IMMANUEL TEAM:** Step two — spread the jam on the bread.

*[Teacher spreads one tiny dot of jam only on the very corner of the bread — technically followed the words, but clearly not a real sandwich.]*

**TEACHER (as robot):** Beep. Jam spread on the bread. Task complete!

*[Hold up the result — wrong in a different way from Nant Team's attempt.]*

**TEACHER (breaking character):** Hmm... is this really a jam sandwich?

*(Students react.)*

**TEACHER:** Great try, Immanuel Team! Now both teams have seen [Robot Name] make a mistake. Let's talk about why.

---

## 7. "Why Did It Fail?" Quick Discussion (3 min, whole group)

**TEACHER:** Let's think together. What happened? Why did [Robot Name] do that?

*(Take answers from both teams.)*

**TEACHER:** That's right! [Robot Name] didn't guess what you meant. [Robot Name] only did exactly what you said — no more, no less. When your instruction wasn't clear, [Robot Name] still followed it exactly — just not the way you wanted.

This is exactly how a real computer works. A computer never guesses your meaning. It only does exactly what your instructions say.

---

## 8. Jam Sandwich — Revise Instructions (3 min, both teams work at the same time)

**TEACHER:** Now, go back to your team and fix your instructions. Think about what went wrong, and make each step clearer. Be specific — say exactly how much, and exactly where.

*[Teacher circulates, helping teams notice vague words like "some jam" or "on the bread" without saying which part of the bread, how much, or with what.]*

**TEACHER:** You have 3 minutes. Go!

> **Teacher prep note (not spoken) — what a complete, correct instruction list looks like, for your own reference while you play the robot:**
> 1. Pick up two slices of bread and place them on the plate.
> 2. Open the jam jar and use the knife to scoop out some jam.
> 3. Spread the jam evenly across the middle of one slice of bread, from edge to edge.
> 4. Place the second slice of bread on top, matching the two slices together.

---

## 9. Jam Sandwich — Retry: Nant Team (3 min)

**TEACHER:** Nant Team, let's try again with your new instructions!

*(Nant Team reads their revised steps. Teacher-as-robot follows them correctly this time and succeeds.)*

**TEACHER (as robot):** Beep. Task complete!

*[Hold up a properly made jam sandwich half.]*

**TEACHER (breaking character):** Wow, look at that! You did it, Nant Team!

*(Cheer together with the students.)*

---

## 10. Jam Sandwich — Retry: Immanuel Team (3 min)

**TEACHER:** Now, Immanuel Team, it's your turn to try again!

*(Immanuel Team reads their revised steps. Teacher-as-robot follows them correctly and succeeds.)*

**TEACHER (as robot):** Beep. Task complete!

**TEACHER (breaking character):** Amazing! You fixed it, Immanuel Team! Both teams — give yourselves a big round of applause!

*[Everyone claps.]*

---

## 11. Whole-Group Debrief (5 min)

**TEACHER:** Let's talk about this together, both teams. What changed between your first try and your second try?

*(Take answers.)*

**TEACHER:** What made your second set of instructions work better?

*(Take answers — guide toward: clearer, more specific, more detailed steps.)*

**TEACHER:** Exactly! When your instructions are clear, in the right order, and specific — that's called an **algorithm**. That's exactly why order and clear instructions matter so much. If instructions are vague, a computer — or a robot — will fail, because it never guesses. It only follows exactly what you say.

---

## 12. Reflection (5 min)

**TEACHER:** Let's take a moment to think on your own. You can say your answer out loud, or write it down.

- What was difficult about giving instructions?
- Why did [Robot Name] fail the first time?

*(Give students a minute of quiet think-time, then invite a few volunteers to share.)*

---

## 13. Closing & Takeaway (5 min)

**TEACHER:** You all did an amazing job today! Let's remember what we learned.

*(Say it together, as a class, pointing to the board:)*

**"Computers never guess. Good instructions make good results."**

**TEACHER:** Just like [Robot Name] today, every robot and every computer only does exactly what we tell it to do — nothing more, nothing less. That's exactly why coding is so important. When you write clear, careful instructions, you can make amazing things happen.

Next time, we'll learn something new — how computers make choices, and how they repeat steps again and again. I can't wait to see you all again!

Thank you so much for today. See you next session!

*[Big smile, wave, close with energy.]*

---

- **Step** — one part of a bigger set of instructions
- **Order** — the sequence steps happen in — order matters!
